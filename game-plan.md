A grid-based micro-stealth game is a fantastic choice for the browser. It combines clear architectural boundaries (grid states) with satisfying gameplay loops. Let’s break this down into a comprehensive, phase-by-phase development plan, detailing the core mechanics, system architecture, and tech implementation.Game Concept OverviewCore Loop: Infiltrate a floor $\rightarrow$ Evade guards/cameras $\rightarrow$ Steal the target asset $\rightarrow$ Reach the elevator to go deeper.Pacing: Ultra-fast restarts. A single mistake sounds the alarm, triggering an instant floor reset.Aesthetic: Clean, high-contrast, or dark-mode minimalist vector art (using primitives like circles for guards and squares for crates) so you can focus entirely on mechanics rather than asset production.Phase 1: Architecture & Technical FoundationsBecause everything is grid-bound, your state machine is the source of truth, and the rendering layer just reflects that state.1. Core State & Data StructuresThe Grid Matrix: A 2D array representing the floor layout.TypeScripttype TileType = 'EMPTY' | 'WALL' | 'OBJECTIVE' | 'EXIT' | 'HIDING_SPOT';
interface Tile {
type: TileType;
hasPlayer: boolean;
occupantId: string | null; // Guard or Gadget
}
Deterministic Game Tick: Movements should happen on a turn-based or time-quantized tick system. When the player clicks an adjacent grid tile to move, a GameTick event fires, processing:Player movement and collision.Guard AI updates (patrol step).Vision/Detection checks.2. Tech Stack RecommendationsEngine/Rendering: Pure HTML5 Canvas or a lightweight 2D WebGL wrapper like PixiJS is perfect here. It keeps the bundle tiny and frame rates locked at 60 FPS.State Management: A clean, decoupled state manager (or single vanilla store class) that separates your game loop logic from your UI overlays (HUD, shop menu, level selection).Phase 2: Core Gameplay Mechanics1. Grid-Based Movement & ControlsPlayer Input: Click an adjacent valid tile or use Arrow/WASD keys. Movement is snappy and instantly snaps to the center of the tile.Hiding Spots: Stepping onto a HIDING_SPOT tile (e.g., a closet or large plant) toggles player.isHidden = true. While hidden, guards cannot detect the player even if they walk directly past or look at the tile.2. Guard Patrols & Vision MechanicsPredictable Patrol Paths: Guards move along a predefined array of grid coordinates (e.g., [(2,3), (2,4), (2,5)]). When they reach the end, they loop or reverse.Directional Vision Cones: Instead of complex 3D raycasting, vision is handled by projecting a "ray" of checked tiles in the direction the guard is facing (e.g., Up, Down, Left, Right).Vision Depth: 3 tiles long.Vision Check: Loop through the tiles ahead of the guard. If a WALL tile is hit, stop checking further in that line. If the player is on one of these checked tiles and !player.isHidden, trigger Alarm State.3. Instant Failure & Reset LoopThe moment detection occurs: freeze input, play an alert animation/sound effect, and trigger level.reset().Critical Detail: Keep the reset time under 300ms to maintain that "just one more try" addictive rhythm.Phase 3: Meta-Progression & The Dopamine LoopTo make it truly addictive, short levels need to feed into a rewarding progression system.1. Economy & UpgradesEvery floor contains loose cash/data drives to collect alongside the primary objective.The Between-Floor Shop: A simple UI screen where players spend collected loot on permanent or consumable upgrades:Gadget - Smoke Bomb: Drop on an adjacent tile to completely blind guard vision cones passing through it for 5 ticks.Gadget - Noise Maker: Throw to a tile within a 3-tile radius to force the nearest guard off their patrol route to investigate for 3 ticks.Passive - Night Vision: Permanently see guard vision cones glowing faintly on the grid.2. Floor ScalingFloors 1–5: Basic patrolling guards, simple linear layouts.Floors 6–10: Introduction of static Security Cameras that rotate 90 degrees every game tick.Floors 11+: Laser barriers that turn on/off every alternating tick, forcing players to time their dashes.Phase 4: Content Generation & ScalabilityYou don’t want to hand-design 50 levels right away.
